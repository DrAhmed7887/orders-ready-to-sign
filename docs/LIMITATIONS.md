# Limitations and Full Audit Trail

This document holds the full audit trail, real-text error rates, recall measurements, synthetic-set details, and protocol caveats moved out of the main [`README.md`](../README.md) so zero transparency is lost.

## Prototype scope & health-record integration

- **Prototype, not a product:** not connected to any health record. A **FHIR R4 reader** is built and tested on synthetic bundles only ([`fhir/leer_fhir.py`](../fhir/leer_fhir.py); `python3 ejecutar.py data/synthetic/fhir/demo_bundle.json --sistema-cie urn:sintetico:cie-10-es`). All six synthetic sets give identical orders after the round trip extract → FHIR → extract (`tests/test_fhir.py`). It has not been run on any real system's export or checked with the official HL7 validator. Standard OMOP concept IDs are not mapped (source codes only). Nothing is written back to the record. The connection to SIA/Abucasis, ECAP or Diraya is designed, not built ([`docs/ARQUITECTURA.md`](ARQUITECTURA.md), in Spanish).
- **Synthetic labels are not blind:** the synthetic sets and their labels were generated by the AI assistant that also wrote the rules, not by a physician, so they are not blind.
- **Baseline filter framing:** the comparison "blood-test-only filter" (last eGFR < 60) is a hypothetical baseline: the Valencian protocol already asks for a yearly ACR. On a single visit the engine's flag and ACR ≥ 30 coincide by construction, so 48 % vs 52 % describes where the markers sit, not a head-to-head test against current practice.
- **Type 1 diabetes coding:** Type 1 diabetes (E10) enters S1a 5 years after its first code (round 13); the date of the first code is not always the date of diagnosis. The NHANES mapping counts all diagnosed diabetes as E11.
- **Combination SGLT2 inhibitors:** ATC `A10BD` combination products are not recognised; only `A10BK`.

## Independent review & round 13 clinical fixes

- **Found by an independent review on 16 Nov 2026 and fixed in round 13** (signed; [`docs/FIRMA_VUELTA_13.md`](FIRMA_VUELTA_13.md), each with a test in [`tests/test_vuelta13.py`](../tests/test_vuelta13.py), all errors in [`docs/ERRORES.md`](ERRORES.md)): two abnormal lab ACRs ≥ 3 months apart now give "persistent: consider a CKD code"; dialysis, transplant, palliative care and recent pregnancy are excluded; one eGFR equation per trend; I12, I13, E11.21 and E11.22 count as CKD codes; a note never removes an eGFR order; impossible values are rejected; the repeat order asks for a first-morning urine; type 1 diabetes is screened from 5 years after its first code. None of these could be tested on real data: NHANES has one visit and no codes beyond diabetes and hypertension.

## What real data (NHANES) did and did not test

- **Partial pipeline execution:** on real data (NHANES) only part of the pipeline runs: the extract, rule S1b, which on one visit is simply ACR ≥ 30 (404 orders for 2,112 people in 2009–2010) and the prepared order. The note reader, S1c and suppression were never run on real data, because NHANES has no notes, drugs or pending orders ([`eval/results/nhanes_trace.md`](../eval/results/nhanes_trace.md)); the workflow diagram marks those steps "Not tested".
- **Unmeasured outcomes:** 3-month persistence (NHANES 2009–2010 second sample is a first-morning void within 10 days), calibration (rules give orders, not scores), non-US populations (Spanish primary care has no public dataset here; cf. Reyna et al. 2020 on held-out hospitals), and clinical outcomes after a signed order (cf. Adams et al. 2022, TREWS).

## Real-text note reader audits (CodiEsp & LivingNER)

- **Development sample (300 CodiEsp cases):** on 300 real published Spanish clinical cases (CodiEsp, CC BY 4.0; [`eval/results/notas_reales.md`](../eval/results/notas_reales.md)) the code accepted 20 findings and 10 were wrong (3.33 per 100 notes, 95 % CI 1.61–6.04; 13 counting three judgment calls), reviewed by an AI agent, not a physician. The sample was enriched for kidney terms (164 of 300 chosen by keyword) and is hospital case reports, so this is not a rate for primary-care notes. Most were "followed in Nephrology" read from a referral or a single consult. None could change an order here, because no accepted finding had a date read from its sentence; in dated notes they could, and a wrong grey line can still mislead the doctor. Attribution: [`eval/notas_reales/NOTICE.md`](../eval/notas_reales/NOTICE.md). How many real findings the reader misses was not measured.
- **First held-out sample (300 CodiEsp cases, round 11 checks):** after that test the checks were tightened (round 11, signed) and measured once on a **fresh held-out sample** of 300 other CodiEsp cases, written down before the run ([`eval/results/notas_reales_heldout.md`](../eval/results/notas_reales_heldout.md)): the code accepted 4 findings and 1 was wrong (0.33 per 100 notes, 95 % CI 0.01–1.84; 2 counting one judgment call), again reviewed by an AI agent, not a physician. Per accepted finding that is 1 wrong in 4 (dev set: 10 in 20), so when the reader does accept something it is often wrong. One held-out case appears to be the same patient as a dev case published twice (text overlap found by an independent review). The wrong one was a hospital admission accepted as Nephrology follow-up because "controlada" (about fluid replacement) matched the follow-up word; left as found and logged for the next round. That sample had no albumin cases left and only 14 Nephrology cases, and the model flagged a done test in none of its 70 creatinine/eGFR cases, so the reader likely misses many real findings (not measured).
- **Second held-out sample (300 LivingNER cases, rounds 13–14 checks):** after rounds 13 and 14 the checks were measured once more on a **second fresh held-out sample**, 300 cases from another public corpus (LivingNER, CC BY 4.0), leaving out anything that duplicates CodiEsp, written down before the run ([`eval/results/notas_reales_heldout2.md`](../eval/results/notas_reales_heldout2.md)): the code accepted 25 findings and 3 were wrong (1.0 per 100 notes, 95 % CI 0.21–2.89; 10 counting seven judgment calls). Per accepted finding that is 22 of 25 correct (15 of 25 on the strict reading). None of the 8 accepted Nephrology follow-ups was wrong, but 3 were judgment calls (a referral "para seguimiento", a discharge with an outpatient appointment, a check-up every 2 years), so 5 of 8 are clear follow-up. The 3 wrong ones: a dipstick "albuminuria ++++" and a 24-hour albumin excretion read as a urine ACR, and "FG > 60" read as exactly 60. None could change an order. Reviewed by an AI agent and re-read by Claude (one verdict changed), both from the same model family and not by a physician.

## Recall & round 15 reading fixes

- **What the reader misses (recall):** measured once on the 300 held-out cases against marks made by AI agents that did not see the model's output (not a physician; Ahmed's check of 20 marks is pending): the code accepted a finding in **3 of 98** notes that had one (3.1 %, 95 % CI 0.6–8.7). It found 3 of 6 Nephrology follow-ups and **none of 94** notes reporting a creatinine or eGFR value ([`eval/results/notas_reales_recall.md`](../eval/results/notas_reales_recall.md)). These are hospital case reports, not the primary-care notes it was built for, but the result means the reader cannot be relied on to find tests already done; it can only add a line when it does. One labelling agent searched for kidney terms instead of reading every sentence.
- **Round 15 (signed 16 Nov; [`docs/FIRMA_VUELTA_15.md`](FIRMA_VUELTA_15.md)):** fixed the reading errors found on held-out 2: a dipstick or 24-hour albumin is no longer taken as an ACR, and "FG > 60" is a bound, not 60. Three blind synthetic sets (160–180 sentences each) were written by AI agents that never saw the code, and each failed its pre-registered test (zero wrong readings); the misses then drove the fixes. Re-scored on the signed code afterwards, so this is descriptive, not a blind result, wrong readings fell from 16, 21 and 28 to 0, 0 and 4. Set 3 still fails. The 4 left are also made by the old code: 3 eGFR bounds that only add a grey line, and 1 ACR with no date. The signed code still reads few ACR values: 8, 1 and 0 of 45. The figures for held-out 2 above describe the round 13–14 checks it was reviewed with. On that text, round 15 removes 2 of the 3 wrong findings and turns the third into a bound note, and adds 2 new unreviewed grey lines with no value. Those texts are where the errors were found, so this is not a new test. A wider value-reading rule (V1) failed all three blind sets and was not adopted.
- **ACR recall (held-out 2 albumin cases):** the finding Amparo's story rests on was measured on the 66 albumin cases of held-out 2 against blind marks by AI agents (not a physician; [`eval/results/notas_reales_recall_acr.md`](../eval/results/notas_reales_recall_acr.md)): 21 cases state an ACR or microalbuminuria result; the code accepted an ACR finding in **5 of 21** (24 %, 95 % CI 8.2–47.2), and in **none of the 5 did it read the value** (two had 57 and 36 mg/g in the sentence next to other urine numbers). No sentence carried a date. So on this real text the persistence path (S1c: an outside ACR ≥ 30 with a date, ≥ 3 months after the lab one) would never have fired; Amparo's case is shown on synthetic notes only. No physician has checked these marks or the 45 albumin cases left unmarked. An accepted ACR finding without a value removes nothing and confirms nothing: it adds a grey line.
- **Prompt-injection boundary:** apart from these tests, the note reader was tested on synthetic Spanish notes only; it fell for 3 notes with instructions aimed at it, and the code rejected them. The defence is a Spanish keyword filter plus the sentence check ([`notas/verificar.py`](../notas/verificar.py)); a reworded attack could pass the filter, so this does not show general resistance to prompt injection.
