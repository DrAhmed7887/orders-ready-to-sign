## What the engine adds to the blood-test filter

- Of people whose second sample is high, the blood-test filter misses 67.2 % (57.7–75.5).
- Of those, the engine orders a repeat for 85.5 % (79.3–90.0).
- Missed per 1,000 people on the list is not the same as the earlier "13.5 per 1,000 not flagged" (1 − NPV): same 28 people, different denominator.
- Found only by the engine: 50.2 (37.3–67.2) per 1,000 people on the list.

## Non-response check (round 13, E3, descriptive)

- High first ACR: 24.6 % of people without a second sample vs 18.6 % with one (unweighted).
- Engine PPV after re-weighting respondents (first-ACR band (<30, 30-44, 45-99, 100-299, >=300) x age 18-39/40-59/60+ x diabetes): PPV 52.8 % (44.2–61.3), sensitivity 87.4 % (81.9–91.4), missed 11.4 (7.7–16.9) per 1,000.

## Not measured

- discrimination: the engine's flag (ACR >= 30 on the first sample) and the reference (ACR >= 30 on the second) are the same test, so sensitivity and specificity are largely 10-day test-retest agreement; the eGFR filter was never meant to detect albuminuria
- persistence over 3 months (KDIGO): the second sample is within 10 days
- calibration: the rules give no scores, only orders
- other countries or record systems: US survey data only (cf. Reyna 2020 on generalisability)
- outcomes after a signed order (cf. Adams 2022, TREWS): no prospective study
- note reader recall
